The island had a fold built for sheep and a head-dyke to keep them off the field, and no sheep.
Now it has a flock, the first thing on it that moves of its own accord.

- **The flock** (`shared/flock.ts`, `features/flock/`). Some seventy sheep on desktop and forty
  on a phone, each on its heft: the brae under the cairn, the inbye above the landing, a few
  come down to the house, four penned in the fold, and two hefts on the far side for `cove`.
  Most are white with black faces and legs, some brown (`moorit`), a few black; a third of the
  ewes on the hill have a lamb at foot. Each goes round two to four places: grazing head down,
  sweeping it across the grass and lifting it now and then to look round, then turning and
  walking a few strides to the next, legs swinging in diagonal pairs, the body pitched to the
  slope. Where a sheep may stand is a rule, read from the land, the path and its waymarks,
  the walls, the field, the house and its door, the trees, the cairn, every postcard's camera,
  and the boulders, which the rocks now lay in `world.clutter` as they place them.
- **Night.** The flock keeps its own clock: it slows as the sun reaches the sea, and each sheep
  lies down where it stood, at its own moment, until morning. The moon finds them as white
  lumps on the dark hill.
- **Trods** (`shared/trods.ts`, `terrain/wear.ts`): narrow ways worn along the level of the
  brae and the inbye, traced across the fall of the land, wavering, stopping at the walls and
  the path. They are the path's own picture's second channel: no new texture, no new program.
- **A postcard**, `flock`: low in the inbye among the sheep, looking up over the head-dyke to
  the brae and the cairn. It is the last stop before `hero`. The day grew by its stop and leg
  (277.5 to 289.5 s) and `OPENING` now follows `DAY`, so second ten keeps exactly its sun.

In `hero` the left hill, which was a plain dark slope with the head-dyke across it, is now
written over with pale trods and flecked with sheep from the shore to under the cairn, and a few
graze by the house. From `landing`, `flock`, `cove` and `infield` they are close enough to see
heads down.

**Why.** The roadmap's two chapters are the inside of the shelter, which `hero` cannot see,
and the night, which no postcard shows. The fold and the head-dyke were waiting for this,
and the trods were on the roadmap's list of ideas.
