# Vision

What this world is for, and where it is going. Two voices: the owner's constraints, which hold
whatever the direction becomes, and the direction itself, which the curator writes and keeps.

## Constraints (the owner's)

The curator works within these and does not rewrite this section.

- **One place, growing.** Each day adds to or deepens the world that is already there. Nothing is
  thrown away for novelty; what stops working is reshaped, not replaced.
- **Made from code.** Shapes, colours, textures and motion are generated. A binary asset is the
  rare exception, small, and argued for in the journal.
- **It runs on a phone.** The mobile tier gets the same world, with fewer of things. A change
  that only looks right on a desktop is not finished.
- **It needs nothing but itself.** No network, no account, no storage a visitor would miss.
- **Quiet by default.** A visitor who touches nothing gets a slow tour of somewhere worth
  looking at. Taking the camera is an invitation, not a requirement.
- **A place anyone can visit.** Nothing violent, sexual or hateful. No real people, brands or
  logos.

## Direction (the curator's)

**What this place is.** A small northern island that someone walked before you. Nobody is ever
seen on it, only what they left: stones stacked on the summit, later a path, a shelter, a light.
A stranger should know it as "the island with the cairn", and now there is a house under it
with its door open on the fire, a boat at anchor, washing on a line, and still nobody.
Beside the landing a pier of the house's stone runs out to deep water, a round tower of the
same stone on its head with a lantern that somebody lights at sunset: a small boat in its
lee, creels, nets drying on poles along the beach, split fish on a rack, gulls over it. A
burn rises at a kerbed spring under the cairn and comes down the brae to the sea beside the
landing: the reason the house, the fold and the landing are where they are. Above the
fold it goes under a mill no bigger than a byre, and turns the wheel under its floor. The
barley is mostly cut: its stacks stand roped before the house's door, one still building, and
a kiln smokes on the shelf by the fold. Beside the house is the only wood on the island,
birch, bent by the wind, its leaves mostly gold and coming down; the trees nearest the door
are stumps, and their logs are stacked under the eave. Below the house is the ground they
worked: a field of rigs, barley and potatoes, walled round with the stones that came out of it,
its gate left open; a fold across the path; and the head-dyke that keeps both from the hill,
running down into the sea. Its sheep are out on the hill, the brae and the inbye flecked with them, white, a few
brown or black, some with a lamb at foot; four are penned in the fold. The starlings come in
over the sea of an evening, wheel over the brae in one cloud, and roost in the head-dyke.
Higher, skeins of wild geese go over, bound south, and do not stop. Off
the east shore is a skerry the tide never covers: someone stacked a small mark on it, and seals
haul out on its slabs, nobody's, and never nearer than that. It lies off a mountain coast: a high fell and a long range in the
haze to the north-west, which nobody goes to, and which the sun goes down behind.

**Mood and light.** One day at the summer's end in the north, turning once in the time the tour takes, so
every stop has its hour. The visit opens on the last of a clear, cool afternoon: a low sun over
the western shoulder, the land in long shadow under an apricot sky. It sets as the tour comes in
to the landing; the walk up is dusk, and the lamp and the window come into their own; the cairn
stands under the first stars; a full moon, never high, lays its path on the sea, and the
merry dancers come and go low in the north; then first
light, a rose sunrise, the morning, and round by a short bright noon to the gold again. The
air is moving but gentle: things sway, nothing thrashes. Calm, a little lonely, never bleak:
the night is blue, not black, and there is always a light to see by.

**Palette.** A handful, all in `src/world/shared/palette.ts`. Cool sky blues and a pale horizon
for distance. Moss and dry-straw greens for the living ground. Warm sand at the edge. Grey stone:
darker for what lies where it fell (`rock`), paler for what a hand has placed (`cairn`). One cold
white (`foam`), where the sea runs out on the land, as first snow on the far tops, and on the gulls. Bare earth (`path`), sand's duller kin,
only where feet have worn the grass away. Two warm notes, both reserved for light itself: the
sun's cream, and the deeper amber of a flame someone lit (`lamp`): the shelter's lamp, the
light at the pier's head and the boat's riding light, and the roads they lay on the water.
One cold green that is nobody's (`aurora`), the merry dancers', a rose over it. Clouds have no colour of their own: the sun's cream where they are
lit, the sky's blues where they are not. Smoke and rain are the same. Weathered wood (`timber`) for what
came by sea and did not grow here: the boat, its oars, its mast; tarred near black where it
keeps the water out, and a net's brown where bark has steeped it. Cloth is the flock's own
wool, undyed, greyed by salt in the sail; linen is the same cream gone toward stone. One pale thing that grows: birch `bark`, near white, scarred with the
rock's grey taken almost to black; its leaves are the meadow's own two greens, going over to the barley's gold made riper (`leaf`), and its cut
wood the bark's colour warmed. One thing sown, `barley`, a pale green-gold, only on the rigs, and
bleached where it is cut (stubble, stooks, stacks), dulled toward earth where it thatches; the potatoes beside it are the meadow's green
gone darker, the furrows bare earth turned darker still.
The hill above the head-dyke wears one colour the meadow does not: `heather`, a dusky mauve,
in bloom on the moor and only there; and under it, on the lower hill, the
dying bracken's rust (`bracken`), in beds between the grazing, never where heather grows. The flock's wool is its own: a dull cream white (`fleece`), a brown (`moorit`) for a few, and
the near black of their faces and legs (`face`), which a black sheep is all over, and the
starlings are too. And one dyed
thing, a worn red (`pennant`): cloth made only to be seen
from the water, and never more than a scrap of it. A new colour needs a job none of these does.
The sky's few roles (zenith, horizon, light, fill, ground) are blended over five hours
(`HOURS`) by the sun's height. At night the light is the moon's cold blue-white, which the stars share.

**Motifs.**
- *Placed stone.* Cairns, markers, walls: the same boulder shape, stacked on purpose. A house
  wall is laid in level courses with its corners locked; a roof is the hill's own turf. A field
  dyke rides the land, three courses and a cope set on edge, a long stone stood up at every
  gap.
- *Wind.* One shared signal that grass, water, clouds, smoke, a boat at its anchor, every
  birch and the barley all read; on the barley it is ripples of paler ears running up the
  field. Steady and gentle; what shows it is that everything answers to it together. The
  trees show it standing still: each grew leaning the way it blows.
- *Cloth.* The wind's loudest voice, hung out and left: the sail slack on its yard, blankets
  and a shirt on the line, the nets, the pennant. It hangs in folds that travel and never
  thrash, and it is thin: a low sun behind it comes through, warmer than it fell.
- *What grows.* Grass, and birch. One wood, where the ground is low and gentle, thinning to
  scrub at its edge; a few trees alone beyond it; none on the cairn's hill. A tree is a pale
  trunk and a few faceted lumps of leaves, never a model of a tree.
- *The high air.* Over the low clouds a thin mackerel sky, combed into streaks by a wind
  aloft that turns from the one below; it thins at night so the stars show between.
- *Showers.* A few heavier clouds trail rain: fine streaks leaning back from the wind. One
  is in the sound as the visit opens; another has just gone over. What they leave is one
  signal (`world.weather`): stone and turf darker and shining with the sky, puddles on the
  path and threads in the furrows, white run-off down the steep faces, the burn in spate,
  the sea pocked under them.
- *The moor.* Above the worked ground the hill is heather, in patches with grass between,
  thickest on the tops: low faceted bushes on a mauve ground.
- *The hour.* The other shared signal (`world.day`): where the sun is, and from that the sky,
  the one light and its shadows, the fill, the haze, the sea's colours and the clouds'. Sun and moon are one light that is out as it changes
  hands; the lamps are the only lights that are somebody's, lit at dusk.
- *The line between.* Shore, ridge, horizon: edges are where the detail goes.
- *The sea gives back.* The water is a mirror, shaken by its ripples and by a low swell: the
  island, the cairn, the boat and the sky lie upside down in it, and the lamp at night. It
  breaks white round whatever stands in it, rides the boat on its swell, and wets the sand
  it runs back off. A gust breaks the picture up.
- *Distance.* What is far is seen through air: fainter, bluer, and fading into the sky's own
  colour on that side. Layers (stacks, headland, range) say how far.
- *The flock.* Sheep, never people: grazing head down, looking round now and then, walking
  a few strides to the next bite, round their heft; lying down as the sun goes, up at first
  light. A sheep is a faceted woolsack, a dark head and four sticks, never a model of one.
  Their trods are worn into the turf as the path is, fainter.
- *The starlings.* One cloud, never a bird you can pick out: it folds, stretches and darkens
  where they bunch. In after noon, into the walls at sunset in a coiling stream, away at
  first light.
- *The geese.* The year's back end, overhead: a ragged chevron behind one bird, out of
  the north and gone over the bay. Nobody's.
- *The seals.* The flock's opposite number on the sea: pale lumps on dark rock, breathing;
  one looks round, one shows its head in the water and is gone.
- *The fishing.* Gear left to dry, never anyone at it: a pier laid like a house wall, a
  light on its head, nets that stir, creels, fish on a rack, a boat to row out in. Gulls
  ride the wind over the bay and stand on its posts by dark.
- *Fresh water.* One burn, found by the land's fall: white where it is steep, a pool by
  the fold, spread over the sand at its mouth.
- *Work left running.* The mill turns, the kiln smokes, the gate stands open, a stack is
  half built with the sledge beside it: somebody is about, and is never seen.
- *Inside.* A room is lit by its own fire and by the day at its door, never by the sun:
  amber at the hearth, dim and cooler by the door. What is in it is what somebody uses.
- *The year.* The back end of it, turning with the work: harvest, the birches gold from the
  outside of the wood in, their leaves torn off by the wind and lying under them, the
  bracken gone to rust.
- *The sward.* Grass by the blade where the visitor stands; far off, the land's grain.
- *Facets for form, grain for surface.* Low-poly and flat-shaded, few colours; a texture on
  every surface: noise in the shader, never a photograph, grain and relief and no colour.

**What it is not.** Not a game: no score, no quest, no characters. Not tropical, not fantasy, no
ruins, no magic glow. Not busy: every added thing must leave the picture as
quiet as it found it. Not photoreal: its textures stop at grain.

**The chapters ahead.** Each is one day's major update. The journal holds the ones done;
the last was what a shower leaves. Next: the other insides.

## Where it starts

A small island in open water under a clear sky: grass, sand, rock, boulders, tufts.
Deliberately plain: a place to begin from, not a style to keep.
